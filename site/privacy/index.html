TweetPicker Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 10, 2026

TweetPicker helps you copy images, download videos and GIFs, and create image cards from posts on X/Twitter.

Information processed

On X and Twitter pages, TweetPicker reads post and media information available to your browser, including post text, author names and handles, images, video URLs, links, dates, and engagement counts. It uses this information to provide its controls and create image cards or find downloadable media. TweetPicker processes this information in your browser; its code does not send it to a developer-operated server or use analytics.

When needed, TweetPicker requests images and public post embed data from X's media and syndication servers. To show the full text of an X Article, it requests that post from X's own API on x.com using your existing X session, the same way the X page does. Those requests can include a post identifier or media URL and expose ordinary network information, such as your IP address, to X. X's own privacy practices apply to your use of its services.

If you choose to copy or download content, the resulting image is placed on your clipboard or the video is saved to your device. Your optional Like and Bookmark settings use X's own controls to perform those actions on your behalf.

Settings and retention

TweetPicker stores your Like, Bookmark, and Expand preferences using Chrome's sync storage. Google may synchronize these settings if Chrome sync is enabled. Post data is held in browser memory for the extension's features; the extension does not persist a history of posts. Copied images and downloaded files remain subject to your device's clipboard and file storage behavior. You can change the settings in TweetPicker and remove the extension through Chrome's extension settings.

Sharing and changes

TweetPicker does not sell user data or use it for advertising, lending, or purposes unrelated to its features. Its use of information received from Google APIs follows the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The developer may update this policy when the extension's practices change; the latest version will be available at the policy URL shown on its store listing.
